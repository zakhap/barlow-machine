Instruments and rhythms. Set how many slots you want (1–8). Each slot gets an instrument, which is the sound: type, pitch, decay, tone, pitch mapping and MIDI channel. It also gets a rhythm, which is the pattern. The patch grid sets which instrument each rhythm plays. Several rhythms can share one conga, and an instrument can sit unused. A rhythm's color is always the color of the instrument it plays.

How it works. Each rhythm is a ring of steps. By default all rhythms share one cycle, so 3, 4 and 5 steps give a true 3:4:5. Each step's petal length is its Barlow indispensability. Barlow split a meter into layers of prime factors (12 = 2·2·3 or 3·2·2…) and ranked every pulse by how much the meter depends on it. In Barlow mode, Density turns pulses on in that rank order, so sweeping it walks down the rhythm tree, and the strata chips change how it branches. In Euclid mode, onsets are spread evenly instead. Lock makes a rhythm avoid (or join) the hits of the rhythms above it, which is how hand-drum ensembles interlock.

Reading a petal. The filled part is its firing chance, the fill brightness is its velocity, and a faint stub is its ghost chance. Tails show the ring-out time, and the dots near the hub trace the pitch. When Strokes is on, weight also picks the stroke: open on strong pulses, muted in the middle, slap on weak ones. Amber values are ones changed from their defaults. A rhythm's Length follows the global Cycle until you change it. It then reads "Custom", runs as a polymeter against the others, and gets its own playhead in the Bloom (marked ′ in the hub). Flipping a step moves Density to match. Drag a knob or value vertically (shift for fine control) and double-click it to reset.
